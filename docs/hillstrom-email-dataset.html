Institutional Context and Provenance

MineThatData published this retail e-mail analytics challenge as a public CSV for analysts to evaluate whether mens or womens e-mail campaigns improved short-run customer behavior. The source post states that the sample contains customers who had purchased within the prior 12 months and were placed into an e-mail test.

Economically, each row represents a prior customer with historical purchasing attributes, randomized campaign assignment, and outcomes measured for two weeks after the campaign.

The treatment is randomized by design: roughly one third of customers were assigned to a mens merchandise e-mail, one third to a womens merchandise e-mail, and one third to no e-mail. This makes simple arm differences interpretable as experimental intent-to-treat contrasts, subject to the source experiment being implemented as described.

Research Notes and Caveats

What the Design Supports

  • Intent-to-treat contrasts between each e-mail arm and no e-mail.
  • Outcome-specific arm selection, such as choosing the best arm by spend.
  • Winner's curse analysis of selected-arm estimates under randomized assignment.
  • Bootstrap or sample-splitting corrections that condition on the selection rule.

What the Design Does Not Support Alone

  • Long-run effects beyond the two-week post-campaign window.
  • Customer-level repeated-outcome analysis without identifiers.
  • Creative-level conclusions about actual e-mail content, because creatives are not included.
  • Profit effects unless margins and campaign costs are supplied separately.

The central empirical risk is not confounding of the campaign arms, because assignment is randomized. The main risk for this repository's winner's curse theme is post-selection overstatement: after choosing the arm with the largest realized lift, the naive selected estimate is upward biased relative to the selected arm's true effect.

Empirical Section Guide

Outcome Definition and Permitted Wording

Use spend as "dollars spent per assigned customer during the two weeks after the campaign." Use visit as "website visit during the two-week window" and conversion as "purchase during the two-week window." Avoid language implying profit, lifetime value, or transaction-level spend.

Treatment Definition

Define the no-email group as the omitted control group. Estimate mens and womens e-mail effects as separate randomized assignment effects. The winner-selection rule can choose the arm with the largest estimated lift in a chosen outcome.

Specification and Identification

A baseline experimental specification for outcome Yi is:

Yi=θm1{Di=Mens}+θw1{Di=Womens}+g0(Xi)+εi.

Here, Di is randomized e-mail assignment, Xi is the vector of pre-treatment customer history controls, g0(Xi) is a baseline outcome function, and θm and θw are intent-to-treat contrasts relative to no e-mail. For machine-learning adjustment, a treatment model can also be learned:

Dki=m0k(Xi)+vki,k∈{Mens,Womens}.

Because assignment is randomized, controls are not required for identification. They can improve precision, support balance diagnostics, and enable heterogeneity or targeting exercises.

Estimator Menu

Estimator family Role in study Expected artifact
Naive selected winner Select the larger realized lift and report it without correction. Baseline overstatement benchmark.
Fixed-arm experimental estimates Report mens and womens contrasts without post-selection conditioning. Transparent arm-level treatment effect table.
Sample splitting Use one split to choose the winner and another split to estimate its effect. Bias-reduced but lower-precision comparator.
Bootstrap correction Estimate and subtract selection-induced bias for the selected arm. Main method comparison for the repository theme.
Covariate-adjusted ML or DML Use historical customer attributes for precision and targeting variants. Robustness table and possible HTE extension.
